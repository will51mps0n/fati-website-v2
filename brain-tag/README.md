# Brain Tag

A Roblox remake of the classic top-down zombie-tag game Braains.io. Humans hide
and barricade, zombies infect them by touch, and a round timer decides the
winner. Everything (map, furniture, characters, UI, NPCs) is generated by
scripts, so nothing has to be placed by hand in Studio.

The art is original (simple parts and colors in a flat cartoon style). The name
"Brain Tag" is used in-game; the original name, logo and sprites belong to their
owner.

## Getting it into Roblox Studio

### From inside Studio (install and update)

1. Open any place in Studio (a new **Baseplate** is fine, or your saved Brain Tag place).
2. Open **View > Command Bar**.
3. Paste this one line into the Command Bar and press **Enter**:

```lua
local H=game:GetService("HttpService") H.HttpEnabled=true local M=Instance.new("ModuleScript") M.Source=H:GetAsync("https://raw.githubusercontent.com/will51mps0n/fati-website-v2/61169082e8c957ff35d5e85ff6e3ae8dc0c60da2/brain-tag/studio/install.luau",true) require(M)()
```

4. Output says `[Brain Tag] installed build ...`. Press **Play**.

Run the same line again whenever the game is updated: it replaces the Brain
Tag scripts with the newest ones. (It also turns on HTTP requests for the
place and removes the template's Baseplate and SpawnLocation, which would sit
on top of the map.) The line points at a fixed copy of the small installer,
which asks GitHub for the newest commit, so it never picks up a stale cached
version; Output shows the build time and commit it installed.

### As a file

The built place is also committed as `BrainTag.rbxlx`. One terminal command
downloads it and opens it in Studio:

- macOS:
  `curl -L -o ~/Desktop/BrainTag.rbxlx https://raw.githubusercontent.com/will51mps0n/fati-website-v2/refs/heads/claude/pensive-mccarthy-jls1ep/brain-tag/BrainTag.rbxlx && open ~/Desktop/BrainTag.rbxlx`
- Windows (PowerShell):
  `iwr https://raw.githubusercontent.com/will51mps0n/fati-website-v2/refs/heads/claude/pensive-mccarthy-jls1ep/brain-tag/BrainTag.rbxlx -OutFile "$HOME\Desktop\BrainTag.rbxlx"; start "$HOME\Desktop\BrainTag.rbxlx"`

Then press **Play**.

### Building it yourself

You need the command-line tool [Rojo](https://rojo.space), installed through
[Rokit](https://github.com/rojo-rbx/rokit).

1. Install Rokit once.
   - macOS / Linux: `curl -sSf https://raw.githubusercontent.com/rojo-rbx/rokit/main/scripts/install.sh | bash`
   - Windows (PowerShell): `Invoke-RestMethod https://raw.githubusercontent.com/rojo-rbx/rokit/main/scripts/install.ps1 | Invoke-Expression`
   - Open a new terminal afterwards so `rokit` is on your PATH.
2. In this `brain-tag` folder run `rokit install` (installs the pinned Rojo, StyLua and luau-lsp).
3. Build the place: `rojo build -o BrainTag.rbxlx`
4. Double-click `BrainTag.rbxlx` to open it in Studio, then press **Play**.

For live editing: run `rojo plugin install` once, then `rojo serve` and click
**Rojo > Connect** in Studio. Saved code changes appear in Studio immediately.

## Testing

- **Alone:** press **Play**. Every round fills up with 10 to 14 bots (Studio and
  live games; fewer as real players join, `Config.BOTS_MIN`, `BOTS_MAX`,
  `MAX_PARTICIPANTS`). They have funny names (Mario Robles, Fart Butt, Poop
  Face, Fat Guy, Booger Bob...), a level from 3 to 6, and the classic look
  (only `Config.BOTS_WITH_SKINS` of them, 3 by default, wear special skins); most
  are 5 or 6, and Mario Robles is a 6. Bots move as sharply as players: full
  speed at once, a dead stop, and instant turns. Zombie bots spread out over
  different humans, lead their target and remember where you hid; a second
  zombie on the same human cuts in from the side. With nobody in sight they
  wander and now and then check a nearby bush (or a room on dark maps). They
  are kept a bit dim on purpose: they only see so far, react a moment late,
  forget you soon and each move at their own pace, so hiding and juking work. A human they
  can't reach draws a crowd that lines up at one door and shoves through
  together; a piece that won't budge for one zombie alone, it walks round.
  They circle round a weapon held out at them, and once hit or held off one
  they stay wary of that human and come at them from behind. Every round
  each human bot takes a role. Builders team up in one room (a fort), split
  its doorways, shove furniture into them (two get behind a piece too heavy
  for one) and lean on the barricade when a zombie arrives. Hiders pick a
  bush of their own just before the outbreak and keep still in it. Runners
  keep away from the zombies. All of them flee toward open ground or through
  doorways, juke sideways when a zombie is about to grab them, and sometimes
  start with a weapon they hold out at zombies. Bots check their whole
  body's width before walking straight anywhere and pathfind otherwise, so
  they don't walk into walls. Turn them off with `Config.BOTS = false`.
- **Several players:** Test tab > Clients and Servers > choose 2 or more players > Start.
- If something breaks, open View > Output and copy the red lines.

### What to try in the first round

1. Read the quick-start card, click **Got it**.
2. Walk into a couch: it should move with you, slower than you walk, and stop
   dead when you stop. A piano or wardrobe won't budge until you're bigger (or
   two of you push together).
3. Push the couch against the inside of a doorway so it covers the gap. Wait for
   the outbreak and check that a zombie bot can't get through.
4. Press **E** on an item (yellow ring) to pick it up. You carry up to 3 items
   (`INVENTORY_SLOTS`): press **1**, **2**, **3** (or click a slot) to choose which
   one is in your hand, **G** drops it.
   A held weapon is solid: walk or turn so it touches furniture to push it.
5. Press **B** to open the shop (hover a card for what it does and its price,
   green if you can afford it, red if not). When the wizard arrives (chat
   message), stand next to him and press **B** for his rare items.
6. As a zombie, press **Z** to jump back to the zombie spawn.

Furniture weight and push feel will need a round or two of tuning. The numbers
are all in `src/shared/Config.luau` (see "Tuning" below).

## Admin panel

Press **F2** (or the red **ADMIN** button on the left) to open it. It is
available to everyone while testing in Studio, to the experience's owner (or
rank 254+ in the owning group) in live servers, and to any UserId listed in
`Config.ADMIN_USER_IDS`. Pick a target (one player, a bot, or everyone), then:

- **Give item:** any item, into their hand or dropped at your feet.
- **Give skin:** any skin (even the alpha zombie); they keep it and wear it now.
- **Speed:** freeze, 0.25x to 3x.
- **Points:** +1,000 / +10,000 / +100,000 / -1,000, or reset to 0 (size and speed follow).
- **Player:** make human or zombie, immunity 100, bring to you, go to them, respawn.
- **Round:** **END ROUND NOW** (straight to the next round), skip the phase,
  +60 / -30 seconds, next map next round, reset furniture, clear ground items.
- **Maps:** **Next: map** plays it after this round; **Now: map** ends this round
  and goes there right away. Your own maps from Studio are listed too.

Admins can also type these in chat:

| Command | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| `/map Haunted` | The next round plays that map (part of a name is enough: `/map christ`) |
| `/map Haunted now` | Ends this round and goes there right away |
| `/endround` | Ends this round now; the next one starts |
| `/maps` | Lists the maps |

The server checks every request, so the panel can't be used by anyone else
even if they find it.

## Maps

Seven maps take turns (`Config.MAP_ROTATION`, two rounds each). Besides
their own furniture, every room gets extra things to shove (small boxes,
boxes, box stacks, crates, long crates, big crates, a table or couch in the
bigger rooms) and yards get crates, barrels and hay bales (pumpkins and
coffins at night, presents in the snow), and most pieces you can push start
up to 7 studs out from the wall so you (and the bots) can get behind them and
shove them into a doorway. Pieces in a corner move out diagonally, and a
small box that still can't get out of a corner is left out (nobody can pull,
so it would only be stuck there). These are placed by `clutter` in
`MapLayout.luau` with a fixed seed, so every server gets the same map, and
they always leave doorways, lanes into each room and the way to every item
spot clear.

- **The Neighborhood:** two big houses across a road and a little one on the island.
- **The Mansion:** one huge house, a pool and a shed.
- **The Haunted House:** night, on a big map. A manor with a cross of
  hallways and about 25 rooms: closets, a cellar and hidden nooks behind
  fake walls (they look like any wall, but you walk straight through; bots
  don't know about them). Eight little outhouses, a crypt, a shed, a pond, a
  graveyard of pushable tombstones and a pumpkin patch full of pumpkins,
  glowing jack-o'-lanterns and hay bales. Every room has a pitch black roof:
  from outside you can't see in, and only the room you're standing in opens
  up for you; each doorway shows a shut door you just walk through. You only
  see around yourself (your lantern), and nobody (bots too) sees into a room
  they aren't in. Cobwebs in the corners hide you like bushes.
- **Christmas Town:** Santa's workshop, four cottages and a gingerbread
  house around a town square with a giant Christmas tree, a frozen pond,
  snowmen and presents everywhere.
- **The Pumpkin Farm** (Halloween, night): a farmhouse, a big barn with
  stalls full of hay, a corn maze (tall corn walls, a new layout every time
  the map file is regenerated, with a few walk-through shortcuts), a pumpkin
  patch, a hay field with scarecrows, a pond and outhouses.
- **The Spooky Village** (Halloween, blood moon): about twenty little
  houses, shops and an inn packed round a market square of pumpkin stalls,
  narrow alleys between them, and a church with a secret wall behind the
  altar and a graveyard out back.
- **Witch Woods** (Halloween, foggy swamp night): a forest of trees you can
  hide in, the witch's hut full of cauldrons, an old mill, four cabins, a
  stone circle, swamps and a pumpkin clearing.

All the Halloween maps are dark (you only see around yourself) and roofed
(you see into a room only once you're in it).

The Haunted House, Christmas Town and the other Halloween maps are generated by `tools/map_plans.py`
(walls, rooms and outdoor things written by hand, furniture fitted in
automatically). To change one in Studio instead, copy it:
`require(game.ServerScriptService.Server.MapStudio).copy("Haunted")`.

## Making your own maps

You build maps right in Studio, out of ordinary parts. Each part's **name**
says what it is; its position and size (seen from above) say where. When the
game starts it turns every map folder into a real map (the same brick walls,
floors, breakable windows and pushable furniture as the built-in maps), and
the bots understand it too.

### Start one

With the game installed, stop playing and paste one of these into the
**Command Bar**:

```lua
require(game.ServerScriptService.Server.MapStudio).new("MyMap")
```
A starter map (320 x 300 studs, about the size of the built-in ones): a house
with two rooms, a cabin, a shed, a road, spawns.

```lua
require(game.ServerScriptService.Server.MapStudio).new("Spooky", "Haunted")
```
The same in a theme: `Classic`, `Haunted` (dark: you only see around
yourself, with roofs over the rooms), `BloodMoon` (dark, red), `Swamp`
(dark, green), `Winter` (snow), `Lab` (white and teal) or `Desert`.

```lua
require(game.ServerScriptService.Server.MapStudio).copy("Mansion", "Castle")
```
A copy of a built-in map (`Neighborhood` or `Mansion`) under a new name, to
change into your own.

```lua
require(game.ServerScriptService.Server.MapStudio).copy("Neighborhood")
```
Keeps the name: your edited copy **replaces** the built-in Neighborhood.

```lua
require(game.ServerScriptService.Server.MapStudio).palette()
```
One of every part and every furniture piece, laid out beside your maps
(folder `_Palette`). Select one, press **Ctrl+D** to duplicate it, and drag
the copy into your map's folder in the Explorer.

Each map is a folder in **Workspace > BrainTagMaps**. The camera jumps to it.
Click **Top** on the view cube (top right of the 3D view) to look straight
down, like the game camera.

### Make it bigger

```lua
require(game.ServerScriptService.Server.MapStudio).resize("MyMap", 400, 360)
```
makes the play area (the green `Bounds` part) 400 studs wide and 360 deep,
keeping its middle in place. You can also select `Bounds` and drag it with
the **Scale** tool. Maps made before this update had the ground locked;
unlock everything once with:

```lua
require(game.ServerScriptService.Server.MapStudio).unlock()
```

### Edit it

Move, resize, duplicate and delete parts with Studio's normal tools. What
each name means:

| Part name | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| `Bounds` | The play area (the big grass plate). A brick wall goes around its edge. One per map. |
| `Wall` | A wall. Draw it long and thin; it is always rebuilt 3 studs thick, centered where you put it. Shotguns can break it. |
| `Door` (green) | Put it on a wall: it cuts a doorway as wide as the part. |
| `Window` (light blue) | Put it on a wall: that stretch becomes a breakable window. |
| `SecretWall` (purple) | Put it on a wall: that stretch looks like normal wall in game, but anyone walks through it. Bots don't know about it. |
| `Floor` | A room's floor. Attribute `Floor`: `Brick`, `Tile`, `Wood`, `Concrete` or `Dirt` (or just set the part's Material). A floor with no walls makes a yard, like the haunted graveyard. |
| `House` | A house's outline (flat plate under the floors): walls on it get blue or orange trim (attribute `Outline`). Optional. |
| `HumanSpawn` (blue) | Where humans appear. At least one. |
| `ZombieSpawn` (red) | Where zombies appear (a red pit). At least one. |
| `Start` (white) | Where everyone starts the round, together. Optional (else the first human spawn). |
| `ItemSpot` (yellow) | Where items can lie on the ground. |
| `WizardSpot` (purple) | Where the wizard can show up. |
| `Bush` | A bush to hide in. Its width is the bush's size. Attribute `Color`: `Green`, `Orange`, or `Cobweb` (an indoor hiding spot). |
| `Road`, `Sidewalk`, `CenterLine`, `EdgeLine`, `Path`, `Patio`, `Pool`, `IslandWalk`, `IslandGrass` | Ground you walk on (pools you can't). |
| `Couch`, `PoolTable`, `Fridge`, `Car`... | Furniture: any piece from the palette. Move and turn it freely. |

Anything else you put in the folder (a statue, a tree, a sign, a toolbox
model) appears in the game exactly as you built it. If it can be bumped into
(`CanCollide` on), it works like a wall that never breaks. Sub-folders are
fine for keeping things tidy.

Folder attributes (select the map's folder, then **Properties > Attributes**):

- `Title`: the name shown in game.
- `Enabled`: untick to leave a map out without deleting it.
- `Theme`: `Classic`, `Haunted`, `BloodMoon`, `Swamp`, `Winter`, `Lab` or `Desert` (colors and lighting).
- `Roofs`: tick to give every room a pitch black roof. From outside you
  can't see in; the room you're standing in opens up for you. Each doorway
  shows a shut door you just walk through.

A folder whose name starts with `_` is ignored. Copying a built-in map keeps
its theme: `copy("Haunted", "MyHauntedHouse")` gives you the haunted house to
change.

### Check and play

```lua
require(game.ServerScriptService.Server.MapStudio).check()
```
prints what each map has (walls, doors, rooms, spawns...) and anything wrong,
like a door that isn't on a wall or a missing spawn. Then press **Play**: your
new maps come first in the rotation, then the built-in ones. In the admin
panel (F2), **Maps** lets you jump to any map (pick it, then **Skip phase**).

Your maps are saved in your place file. Updating the game with the Command
Bar line never touches them.

## Saving progress

Best score, wins, skins and purchases save with DataStores. Points do not:
everyone starts at 0 each time they join. Saving needs two things:

1. Publish the place: File > Publish to Roblox.
2. Home > Game Settings > Security > turn on **Enable Studio Access to API Services**.

Until then the game runs normally but prints one warning in Output and does not
save. If loading someone's data ever fails, that session is never saved, so a
hiccup can't wipe real progress.

## How it plays

| Phase | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| Waiting | Fewer than 2 players (bots count): "Waiting for players". |
| Prep (60 s) | Everyone is human. "Outbreak happening in N seconds". Shop, grab items, push furniture into doorways. The wizard shows up at a random moment. |
| Turning (last 10 s of prep) | The first zombies are picked at random, weighted by immunity (pills). Their names go red for everyone, they slow down (`TURNING_SLOW`), their own screen says "You turn into a zombie in N seconds!", and chat warns everyone. Bots run from them. |
| Outbreak | They turn into zombies right where they stand (set `FIRST_ZOMBIES_TO_SPAWN` to send them to the zombie spawn instead). |
| Infection (180 s) | The first zombies get the alpha look (orange, gray face, red claws). A single touch from any part of a zombie (body or arms) converts a human and takes 75% of their points; the human's item is lost. Every human still standing gets +10 points every 5 seconds (a little "+10" floats up over you), and every zombie loses 2% of its points on the same beat (`ZOMBIE_DECAY_FRACTION`), so zombies shrink unless they eat. Late joiners spawn as zombies. Weapons only shove and stun. Walk into a bush to hide: it covers you, your name tag disappears, and bots can't see you unless they come close. |
| End (6 s) | "Survivors Win!!!" if anyone survived the timer, "Zombies Win!!!" if nobody did. Rewards, growth. |
| Reset | Furniture, walls and items reset; everyone respawns as a human, all together at the map's start spot. |

Score (points) starts at 0 every time you join and carries through the
rounds while you stay in the server. It buys items and sets your speed and size, which
update the moment your points change (bottom-left shows points, speed boost
and size). A zombie that eats you takes 75% of your points
(`INFECT_STEAL_FRACTION`) plus a small flat bonus (`INFECT_REWARD`), so
eating a big player is worth a lot and zombies grow as they feed. Speed multiplier =
1 + score / 30,000 (capped at 1.6). Size = 1 + score / 8,000 and keeps growing
(up to `MAX_SCALE` = 8, at 56,000 points), so a 12,000-point player is about
2.5x a newcomer, like in the original, and the top players get huge. Only the
collision box stops growing (`CHARACTER.MaxRootWidth`), so giants still fit
through every door; held items grow with you up to 2.7x. Bigger
players push heavier furniture faster: each pusher's strength is size squared
(zombies are 1.5x stronger, `ZOMBIE_STRENGTH`, and 15% faster,
`ZOMBIE_SPEED_FACTOR`), and a piece moves at walk speed x (1 - mass / total
strength). Furniture keeps a little space from you (an invisible bumper,
`CHARACTER.BumperDiameter`) and starts moving `PUSH_REACH` studs before it
reaches it, so you push from a short way off instead of walking into it.
Hold **F** (L2 on a gamepad, GRAB on a phone) next to a piece to grab it,
like in the original: it comes with you, pulled or pushed, keeping its place
beside you, and you let go when you release F or it snags on a wall. Heavy
pieces slow you down and a massive one won't come alone. Humans only. Builder
bots grab pieces too and carry them into their doorways.
Furniture is solid to weapons too: a piece pushed into your weapon (or your
weapon pressed into one that can't move) pushes you back, so the weapon
bounces off instead of sinking in, and nobody is ever left inside a piece. Light, medium,
heavy and massive pieces (piano, wardrobe, pool table, car) need more and more
strength. Pushing is simulated on your own machine while you are the only one
pushing a piece, so it responds instantly; when someone pushes back, the server
settles the tug of war.

### Items (score prices)

Melee weapons can't hit: they only keep people away while you hold them out.
The one exception is the super rare, Robux-only Ancient Spear, which can also
strike and send zombies flying (`HITTERS` in `Items.luau`).
Items are rare on the ground (1 to 2 a round, only Bats and Swordfish, and an
eaten human's item is lost), so you mostly buy them; each bot has a 20% chance to start with one
(`BOT_WEAPON_CHANCE`). A held weapon is solid: furniture it touches is pushed out of the way, and anyone who walks
into it, zombie or human, is shoved back, so a weapon held across a doorway keeps them out.
Each weapon has its own firmness and thickness (`SOLIDITY` in `Items.luau`):
the bat is short but holds zombies firmly, the pool noodle is long and fat but
soft, so zombies squeeze past it, spears are thin. A bigger holder holds
harder.

## Monetization

Three layers, from smallest to biggest commitment. All are wired up and only
need ids from the Creator Dashboard (see "Setting up Robux items" below).

**1. Small one-off purchases (developer products).** Instant Bat, Instant Spear,
Instant Shotgun, the super rare **Ancient Spear** (Robux only, and the only
weapon that can strike), all delivered straight to your hand (or saved for next
round if you are a zombie), Immunity Shot (+50), and a 2x Score boost for 30
minutes.

**2. Passive buffs (game passes).** VIP (gold name, [VIP] chat tag, +20% score,
VIP-only skins), Double Score, Starter Kit (a Bat every round), Lucky (rare roll
chance 5% to 15%).

**3. Skins.** Human, for score: Chef, Ninja, Pumpkin, Bear, Cat, Frog, Bunny,
Donkey, Koi, Penguin, Cowboy, Pirate, Knight. Animal skins are whole animals
seen from above (a fluffy bear, a cream dog with floppy ears and a curled
tail, an orange and white koi...), not a person in a hat. Human, for Robux: Dog, Panda, Robot, Alien,
Shark, Awesome Guy, Duck, Astronaut. VIP: Royal. Zombies all look the same (the
round's first zombies get the alpha look), so there are no zombie skins. Score
skins give non-paying players something to grind for; Robux skins are pure
cosmetics, the safest thing to sell.

| `Product_SkinAlien` | Alien skin | Product | 79 |
| `Product_SkinAwesome` | Awesome Guy skin | Product | 99 |
| `Product_SkinAstronaut` | Astronaut skin | Product | 99 |

Anything without an id is hidden from the Robux tab in live servers; in Studio
it shows "Needs an id (README)" so you can see the layout. Studio purchases are
test purchases and cost nothing. (The ids can also go straight into
`Config.MONETIZATION` in `src/shared/Config.luau`, but the attributes win and
survive updates.)

Purchases are granted once and recorded by purchase id in the buyer's saved
data before Roblox is told they succeeded, so a server crash or retry can't
double-grant or lose a purchase. Robux never buys a random roll (rare rolls only
happen on score purchases), which keeps you clear of paid-random-item rules.
Bots never get rare or expensive items: they only start with (or pick up)
cheap common ones (`BOT_MAX_ITEM_PRICE`).

Two zero-code options worth turning on: **Premium Payouts** (Roblox pays you for
time Premium members spend in the game) and paid **Private Servers** (in the
experience's settings).

A fairness note: score also sets speed, so Double Score and the 2x boost make
paying players slightly faster over time. The speed cap (1.6x) limits this. If
it feels unfair, lower `SPEED_MULTIPLIER_CAP`.

## Tuning

Everything lives in `src/shared/Config.luau`:

- **Furniture feel:** `FURNITURE_MASS` (how hard each weight class is to move;
  lower = easier), `PUSH_MAX_FRACTION` (fastest a piece can go, as a share of
  walk speed), `FURNITURE_SPIN` (how
  much off-center pushes turn a piece). "Can't move the fridge": lower `Heavy`.
- **Size:** `SIZE_MULTIPLIER` and `MAX_SCALE`.
- **Maps:** `MAP_ROTATION` and `ROUNDS_PER_MAP` (your own maps from Studio play first; see "Making your own maps").
- **Rounds:** `PREP_SECONDS`, `ROUND_SECONDS`, `ZOMBIE_RATIO`, rewards, `SURVIVAL_POINTS` / `SURVIVAL_POINTS_SECONDS` (points for staying alive).
- **Zombies:** `ZOMBIE_SPEED_FACTOR`, `ZOMBIE_STRENGTH`.
- **Push distance:** `PUSH_REACH` and `CHARACTER.BumperDiameter`.
- **Camera:** `CAMERA_HEIGHT`, `CAMERA_FOV`.
- **Collisions:** `PLAYERS_COLLIDE` (characters bump into each other), `CHARACTER.ShellDiameter`.
- **Held weapons:** `SHAFT_SOFTNESS`, `FIELD_WEAPON`, `FIELD_RECOIL` (how hard a weapon and the people pressing on it push each other, shared by size), `SHAFT_FURNITURE_FACTOR`, and per item `SOLIDITY` in `Items.luau`.
- **Furniture size:** `FurnitureSpecs.SIZE_SCALE` (and `KEEP_SIZE` for pieces that sit against walls).
- **Points:** `INFECT_STEAL_FRACTION`, `GROUND_ITEMS_MIN` / `GROUND_ITEMS_MAX`.
- **Bushes:** `BUSH_HEIGHT`, `BUSH_REVEAL_DISTANCE` (how close a bot must get to spot you), `BUSH_SEE_THROUGH`.
- **Sounds:** about 40 effects (swings, bonks, squeaks, the classic "oof" when
  you're eaten, countdown ticks, footsteps, sprays, zombie groans, UI clicks,
  win/lose chimes) are built in `src/client/SoundBank.luau` from the sound files
  that ship with Roblox, so they always play with no uploads. To replace one
  with an audio asset you own, add it to `Config.SOUND_OVERRIDES` by name.

Furniture never slides: it moves only while pushed. Opposite pushes cancel, and
a piece pressed against walls stays put. That is what makes a barricade hold.
